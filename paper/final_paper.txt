In this paper we explore building an accurate toxicity classifier for in-game chat that is fast enough to run in a real-time overlay. Modern gaming language is structurally very different from everyday text: heavy use of slang, acronyms, and game-specific vocabulary, combined with very short messages, makes it challenging for general-purpose language models to recover the meaning of an utterance. We treat this as an intent classification task, assigning each message to one of four classes — (E) Explicit Toxicity, (I) Implicit Toxicity, (A) Action, and (O) Other — using a DistilBERT model fine-tuned on the CONDA dataset of labelled Dota 2 chat. We additionally test whether a normalised game-time scalar, prepended to each message as [TIME=x.xx], can compensate for the loss of conversational context that occurs when classifying messages one at a time, and report a small but consistent negative result.
